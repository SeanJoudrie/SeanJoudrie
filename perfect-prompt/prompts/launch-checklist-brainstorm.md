# The idea
A "launch-readiness" guide for people who vibe-coded an app with AI tools and now want to release it. I'm one of those people. It doesn't review the finished product, and it doesn't teach people how to build. It tells them what they still need to do before they can ship, step by step.

How I picture it working:
1. The user describes their app. Two ways to do it:
   - I give them a short prompt to paste into their own AI chat, and it writes a summary of the app for them.
   - They paste their code. This would be optional and not recommended, because then I'd have access to their code.
2. Dropdown menus narrow it down. First the kind of product (app, website, and so on), then the category (finance, health and wellness, nature, and so on), then more detail from there.
3. It works out which extra steps apply to that category. For example, a finance app probably has more requirements than a nature app.
4. It gives them a gap analysis: "Here are your biggest problems. No terms of service. No secure backend or encryption. No privacy policy." And so on.
5. It turns the gaps into an ordered checklist to get into the App Store. Step 1: write a privacy policy. Step 2: write terms of service. And so on. It says *what* to do, not *how* to build it.

Why I think there's a market: people are paying real money to figure out how to release their apps. I'm hoping this could make me some money too.

# What I want from this chat
- Pressure-test the idea. Who exactly pays for this, why would they pay, and how much? What already exists, and what would make mine different?
- Help me map the checklist. Start with the requirements every app has, then the extra ones by category (finance, health, kids, apps with user accounts or payments). Name which ones are real App Store or legal requirements and which are just good practice. When you're not sure a requirement is current, say so. Rules change, and I'll verify them before building anything on them.
- Poke at the risky parts:
  - Could people rely on this as legal or compliance advice, and what does that mean for me?
  - How do I handle pasted code safely, or should I avoid it entirely?
  - Is a user's own description of their app accurate enough to find real gaps?
- Help me find the smallest version worth launching, and what should wait until later.
